简明英文样例答案 / Simple English model answer

Place a radiation detector at a measured distance d from the source and use a counter and timer to find the count rate. Take readings at at least five distances, with the largest at least four times the smallest. Keep every distance above 5 cm to stop alpha particles reaching the detector, or use a paper filter. Measure and subtract the background count rate. Count for a long time, repeat readings and take means. Handle the source with tongs and store it in a lead-lined box when not in use. Plot corrected count rate C against 1/d². A straight line through the origin supports the inverse-square law; a constant distance correction may explain an intercept.
